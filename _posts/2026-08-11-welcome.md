This is my study log.

Over the next ~10 months I'm working through a roadmap that starts at
matrix multiplication and ends at a deployed AI agent — the math, then
classical ML, then deep learning, then transformers built from scratch,
fine-tuning, RAG, and agents. Everything I learn gets written up here, the
same day I learn it.

## Why write it down

Because I can't fake understanding in prose.

It's easy to watch a video, follow along, feel like it landed, and discover a
week later that nothing stuck. Writing an explanation is the cheapest test I
know for whether I actually understood something — the gaps show up
immediately, in the sentence I can't finish.

That's the whole reason this blog exists. The audience is a nice side effect;
the real function is that "I'll have to explain this tomorrow" changes how I
watch a lecture.

There's a second reason: a public log is a portfolio that builds itself. Every
checkpoint I hit becomes a post and a commit, and at the end of it there's a
trail of work rather than a list of courses I claim to have finished.

## What's here

Two kinds of posts:

**Daily study logs.** One per study session, collected under
[AI Fundamentals]({{ '/ai-fundamentals/' | relative_url }}). Notes cleaned up
into something readable, with the sources at the bottom so you can go to the
original. These are notes-in-public, not tutorials — I'm learning this
material, not teaching it from expertise.

**Project write-ups.** The roadmap is built around checkpoints — things I have
to build to prove a phase actually landed, not just watch. An autograd engine
from scratch. A CNN on CIFAR-10. A tokenizer. A GPT trained on a dataset I
collected myself. A RAG pipeline with a real eval set. Those get longer posts
with the code, the numbers, and what went wrong.

## The rules I set myself

- **No copy-paste.** After a video or an article, close it and reproduce the
  idea from memory. The struggling part is the learning part.
- **Every phase ends in something built.** Video completion is not progress.
  A working checkpoint is.
- **Evals before features.** Once I'm building AI systems rather than studying
  them, nothing gets called an improvement without a number behind it. "It
  feels better" is not a result.
- **Write up the failures too.** Loss curves that don't converge, fine-tunes
  that make the model worse, RAG systems that confidently cite the wrong
  chunk. Those are the posts I'd want to read, so those are the posts I'll
  write.

If you're on a similar path and I've got something wrong, tell me. Being
corrected early is considerably cheaper than being wrong for six months.
